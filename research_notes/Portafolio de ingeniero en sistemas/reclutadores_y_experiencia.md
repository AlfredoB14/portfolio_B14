# Recruiters, hiring managers and how to present experience, education, certifications and achievements on a junior software engineer's portfolio (Mexico/LatAm + international remote, as of Nov 2026)

## Q1. How much does a portfolio matter vs. résumé, LinkedIn and GitHub for junior engineers in 2025–2026, and what changed with AI-generated applications and AI-based screening?

### Takeaway
For juniors the résumé (and, in Mexico, the job-board profile) is still the gatekeeper. The portfolio site works as a verification layer, not a replacement. In OCC's 2026 Mexico study only 33% of recruiters look at a "professional profile or portfolio" in the first screen, against 92% who look at experience. Hiring-side voices say portfolio sites often get "barely glanced at", and they click GitHub or commit history first. Two things changed in 2025–2026. First, AI-written applications flooded pipelines and damaged trust: 74–76% of hiring managers report AI résumés and authenticity problems, 67% of US HR leaders say AI-generated applications slowed hiring, and Gartner forecasts 1 in 4 fake candidate profiles by 2028. Second, recruiters adopted AI sourcing agents. As a result, proof that is hard to fake and consistent across channels has gained value, and generic AI-written text has become a liability. Entry-level is the weakest hiring segment, so "can deliver immediately" signals matter most.

### Inferences
- **Role of the site.** For a junior, the portfolio is the place where a skeptical reader *verifies* the résumé, LinkedIn and job-board claims. It does not replace them. The site's first screen should confirm the résumé story in seconds and offer deeper proof one click away.
- **What gained value.** AI has inflated text, so value has moved to artifacts that are costly to fake:
  - deployed demos that work
  - repos with real commit history, tests and CI
  - open-source PRs merged by other maintainers
  - case studies showing reasoning and trade-offs
  - consistent identity and dates across the CV, LinkedIn, GitHub and the site

  Generic, AI-sounding copy is now a negative signal.
- **LinkedIn consistency.** LinkedIn's AI agent sources from profile data, so LinkedIn must carry the same titles, dates, skills keywords and portfolio URL as the CV. In Mexico, the OCC and Computrabajo profile plays the same role, since job boards are recruiters' main channel.
- **Referrals.** Recruiters in Mexico source through personal references (34%) far more than social media (8%), and Orosz stresses referrals. The portfolio link is most useful when a referrer forwards it, so it should be short, memorable and make a strong first impression.
- **AI/automation profile.** Developers themselves distrust AI accuracy, so an AI/automation portfolio should show *evaluation*: test sets, accuracy against human labels, failure handling, cost and latency, and human-in-the-loop design. "Built a chatbot" alone is not enough.
- **Entry-level signals.** Entry-level hiring is flat and employers reportedly want immediate value. Production experience (internal tools with real users, shipped features, on-call or support exposure) should lead over academic or tutorial projects.

### Gaps
- No rigorous 2024–2026 survey found that measures how often recruiters or hiring managers open a candidate's portfolio site vs. GitHub vs. LinkedIn. Blog statistics such as "73% of hiring managers consider a portfolio more important than a résumé" or "hiring managers spend ~15 seconds on a portfolio" could not be traced to original surveys and should not be used.
- Several figures appeared only in search summaries without a reachable primary page, so they were excluded from Cited Findings:
  - Greenhouse 2025 AI in Hiring report: "65% of hiring managers caught applicants using AI deceptively"
  - Robert Half Nov 2025: "65% of 2,000+ US hiring managers say AI-enhanced applications make verifying skills harder"
  - A Bay Area survey: "60% want proof of AI skills via interviews, tasks or real work"
- The Marlow & Dabbish sample size could not be checked because the PDF text was unreadable.
- No Mexico-specific data found on whether Mexican recruiters open GitHub or portfolio links. The OCC "profile or portfolio" category (33%) mixes the job-board profile with the portfolio.

## Q2. How do recruiters vs. technical hiring managers consume a portfolio (time, first clicks), what builds credibility, and what are the red flags?

### Takeaway
No rigorous study of time spent on portfolio websites exists. The most-cited number, Ladders' 7.4 seconds, applies to résumés, comes from 2018, and has been criticized as methodologically opaque. The consistent pattern is this:
- Non-technical screeners skim titles, keywords, dates and fit very fast.
- Technical hiring managers "read between the lines". When they click, they go to GitHub, commit history, open-source contributions and evidence of real problem-solving.
- Credibility comes from verifiable, iterated, real-user work with numbers.
- Red flags: tutorial clones, vague commits, cluttered or multi-column layouts, missing headings, generic AI text, long certificate lists without built work, and identity or date inconsistencies.

### Cited Findings

**Time and scanning behaviour**
- Ladders eye-tracking study (2018, reported by HR Dive Nov 8, 2018) [OLDER]:
  - Recruiters skimmed résumés for "an average of 7.4 seconds", up from about 6 seconds in 2012.
  - Résumés that succeeded "featured simple layouts, with clear sections and heading titles".
  - E- and F-pattern layouts did well "especially when they utilized bold titles and bulleted accomplishments".
  - Cluttered layouts, too little white space, multiple columns, long sentences and missing section or job headers performed poorly.
  
  [F] — [HR Dive](https://www.hrdive.com/news/eye-tracking-study-shows-recruiters-look-at-resumes-for-7-seconds/541582/)
- Critique by Susan Gygax (ERE, Oct 20, 2020): the 2018 report does not disclose the number of recruiters, their experience, position types, résumé lengths or repetitions, and is essentially a guide to making résumés easier to read. No study measures recruiter review time well, and review time likely varies by job type. [F] — [ERE](https://www.ere.net/articles/is-the-6-second-resume-scan-a-myth)
- Nielsen Norman Group: the "layer-cake" pattern, where the eye jumps between headings and subheadings, is described as by far the most effective way to scan pages apart from reading nearly every word. It was first documented in 2004. [S] — [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/layer-cake-pattern-scanning/)

**Who reads, and how**
- Orosz [OLDER, 2020]:
  - Hiring managers are usually the most technical readers and the most likely to "read between the lines".
  - HR generalists check overlap with the job description.
  - Recruiters do most screening at mid-size companies.
  - Inbound sourcers at large companies follow instructions such as specific languages or years of experience.
  - Interview panels skim the résumé before interviews.
  
  [F] — [Orosz, dev.to](https://dev.to/gergelyorosz/six-principles-your-resume-should-follow-so-recruiters-will-read-it-3a0o)
- Tech Interview Handbook (updated Aug 7, 2026): screeners "are in fact quickly scanning for keywords of skills or experiences that they value", and ATS rules can auto-reject candidates. [F] — [Tech Interview Handbook](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/resume/)
- Ask HN (Feb 2025):
  - purvesta looks at git history and commit messages, and at open-source contributions to established projects, because they went through "a robust review process". He is impressed by one project iterated over "3/6/12 months" with refactors and a release. Vague commits like "Updated stuff" are a red flag.
  - gregjor values a "history of growth and accomplishments" and solving business problems. Red flags: tutorial-style to-do apps, and candidates who don't know what the company does.
  
  [F] — [Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43134745)
- Underdog.io (vendor, 2026): a hiring manager seeing a GitHub link may click it immediately to form a quick impression. [S] — [Underdog.io](https://underdog.io/blog/what-hiring-managers-look-for-on-github)

**Credibility signals**
- Orosz's advice for people with less experience:
  - Publish projects with strong READMEs (summary, screenshots, run and test instructions).
  - Add automated tests and mention coverage.
  - Contribute to popular open-source projects and cite the project's user base.
  - Polish take-home challenges into public projects.
  - Use numbers: team size, users, adoption, revenue.
  
  [F] — [Orosz, dev.to](https://dev.to/gergelyorosz/six-principles-your-resume-should-follow-so-recruiters-will-read-it-3a0o)
- InfoWorld (Oct 27, 2025): Watts (8x8) values "attitude and aptitude" and what candidates have built and solved. Miller (IFS) says employers want "proof you can deliver". [F] — [InfoWorld](https://www.infoworld.com/article/3709508/do-programming-certifications-still-matter.html)
- HackerRank 2025: 96% of developers say problem-solving should matter more than memorization, and 66% prefer practical coding challenges. Tech leaders rank "ensuring assessments are relevant" as their top concern. [F] — [HackerRank 2025 DSR](https://www.hackerrank.com/reports/developer-skills-report-2025)
- Marlow & Dabbish [OLDER]: employers favour activity traces that are hard to manipulate and cheap to check. [S] — [Marlow & Dabbish PDF](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~xia/resources/Documents/Marlow-cscw13.pdf)

**Red flags and common mistakes**
- **Generic AI-written content.** 33.5% of hiring managers say they spot AI résumés in under 20 seconds, 19.6% would reject them, and 62% say uncustomized AI résumés are more likely to be rejected. [S] — [TopResume](https://topresume.com/career-advice/ai-in-hiring-survey); [Resume Now](https://www.resume-now.com/job-resources/careers/ai-applicant-report)
- **Layout problems.** Clutter, no white space, multiple columns, long sentences and missing headers (Ladders). [F] — [HR Dive](https://www.hrdive.com/news/eye-tracking-study-shows-recruiters-look-at-resumes-for-7-seconds/541582/)
- **Tutorial projects and vague commits.** To-do apps and tutorial clones; commit messages like "Updated stuff". [F] — [Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43134745)
- **Certificate lists without work.** Watts is wary of "a laundry list of certifications" with little real-world evidence. [F] — [InfoWorld](https://www.infoworld.com/article/3709508/do-programming-certifications-still-matter.html)
- **Formatting overuse.** "Overusing highlighting or bolding defeats the purpose"; Orosz also advises no photo, in a US/UK tech context. [F] — [Orosz, dev.to](https://dev.to/gergelyorosz/six-principles-your-resume-should-follow-so-recruiters-will-read-it-3a0o)
- **Inconsistent identity details.** Candidate fraud has made these a risk signal: Gartner tells employers to add identity verification and anomaly alerts. [S] — [HCA Mag](https://www.hcamag.com/ca/news/general/ai-in-hiring-undermines-jobseekers-trust-report-finds/544681); [Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-07-31-gartner-survey-shows-just-26-percent-of-job-applicants-trust-ai-will-fairly-evaluate-them)

### Inferences
- **First screen of the site (the "recruiter path", about 5–10 s).**
  - Name and target role, e.g. "Junior Software Engineer · Full-stack / Backend & Data / AI Automation".
  - Location and availability: "City, Mexico · UTC−6 · Remote / hybrid".
  - Languages: "Spanish native · English C1".
  - 1–3 featured items with one metric each.
  - Visible actions: Download CV (EN/ES), Email, LinkedIn, GitHub.

  Use headings and short bolded labels so layer-cake scanning works.
- **The "hiring-manager path" (one click deep).** Each featured project gets a case-study page with:
  - problem and users
  - constraints
  - what I built and my exact role
  - an architecture diagram
  - key decisions and trade-offs
  - metrics and results
  - tests and CI
  - what I'd do next
  - links to the live demo, repo and a short video if the demo needs login
- **Proof by profile:**
  - **Full-stack web:** a working deployed app with demo credentials or a guest mode, auth, a real database, responsive and accessible UI, performance budget or Lighthouse numbers, and e2e tests.
  - **Backend/data:** API docs (OpenAPI), data model, volume handled (rows, requests, files), pipeline scheduling, idempotency and retries, observability, and tests.
  - **AI/automation:** an evaluation set and accuracy against a baseline or human labels, cost and latency per run, guardrails and fallbacks, time saved for real users, and prompt or agent versioning.
- **Things to avoid:**
  - broken links or expired free-tier demos
  - placeholder text
  - self-rated skill bars or percentages (no evidence supports them; they read as unverifiable claims)
  - ten shallow projects instead of 2–4 deep ones
  - AI-boilerplate "passionate developer" copy
  - dates that differ from LinkedIn
- **Writing quality.** Clear case studies and READMEs act as a proxy for communication skill, which matters especially for remote roles.

### Gaps
- No eye-tracking or click-stream study of developer portfolio websites was found. Time per portfolio and first-click order for recruiters vs. hiring managers cannot be quantified with good sources.
- A 2025 "InterviewPal" figure of 11.2 seconds of initial scan with AI-assisted tools appears only in career blogs. Low confidence; not used.
- Orosz's book *The Tech Resume Inside Out* was not accessed. His 2020 article is used instead.
- The Ladders 2018 study PDF (csuglobal.edu mirror) returned 404, so the finding that "recruiters looked longest at job titles" could not be verified.

## Q3. Best way to present professional experience on a website (layout, bullet formula, bullets per role, tech tags, promotions, junior metrics, NDA/internal tools)

### Takeaway
Use a reverse-chronological, single-column list (a "light timeline"). Each role gets a scannable header (role · company with a one-line descriptor · location/remote · dates), then a few impact bullets in Google's XYZ form, with technologies at the *end* of the entry. Stack promotions under one company header. Juniors should quantify with honest proxies: users, time saved, volume, error rate, latency, adoption, tests. For confidential or internal tools, describe the problem, users, technical pattern and *relative* impact without client names or proprietary figures. The layout evidence comes from résumé and web eye-tracking (clear headings, bullets, single column). No portfolio-specific study compares timeline vs. cards vs. list.

### Cited Findings

**Layout evidence**
- Ladders [OLDER]: simple layouts with clear sections and headings, bold titles and bulleted accomplishments did well. Multiple columns, clutter and long sentences did poorly. [F] — [HR Dive](https://www.hrdive.com/news/eye-tracking-study-shows-recruiters-look-at-resumes-for-7-seconds/541582/)
- NN/g: clear headings and subheadings enable the layer-cake scan, the most effective scanning pattern. Secondary summaries advise putting keywords at the start of headings. [S] — [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/layer-cake-pattern-scanning/)
- Orosz [OLDER]: "clear, top-to-bottom layout" similar to LinkedIn, with bold and whitespace used sparingly. [F] — [Orosz, dev.to](https://dev.to/gergelyorosz/six-principles-your-resume-should-follow-so-recruiters-will-read-it-3a0o)

**Bullet formulas**
- Laszlo Bock (then Google SVP of People Operations, LinkedIn post 2014, "My Personal Formula for a Winning Resume") [OLDER, still the canonical reference]: "Accomplished [X] as measured by [Y] by doing [Z]".
  - Bock stresses a number and a baseline or comparison, and contrasts a plain duty ("Wrote editorials for The New York Times") with a quantified achievement ("Had 50 op-eds published…").
  - Auburn's accountancy blog notes the formula is a guide, not a sentence to paste verbatim.
  
  [S] — [Business Insider India](https://www.businessinsider.in/google-hr-boss-says-this-is-the-key-to-a-perfect-resume/articleshow/43924179.cms); [Quartz](https://qz.com/201088/google-just-revealed-the-incredibly-simple-formula-for-killer-resumes); [Auburn](https://harbert.auburn.edu/blogs/school-of-accountancy/better-resume-formula.html); [Cal State LA copy](https://www.calstatela.edu/sites/default/files/formula_for_a_winning_resume.docx)
- Orosz [OLDER]:
  - "Accomplished {impact} as measured by {number} by doing {specific contribution}".
  - Use active verbs (led, drove, improved).
  - "Impact and your contribution are more important to convey than the technologies"; name the technologies near the end of each entry.
  
  [F] — [Orosz, dev.to](https://dev.to/gergelyorosz/six-principles-your-resume-should-follow-so-recruiters-will-read-it-3a0o)
- Tech Interview Handbook (Aug 2026):
  - Template: "[Accomplishment summary]: [Action] that resulted in [quantifiable outcome]".
  - List the "Scope of job and skills required".
  - "Highlighting a few of your best achievements is better than including many 'average' achievements". It gives no fixed number of bullets.
  
  [F] — [Tech Interview Handbook](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/resume/)
- STAR (Situation-Task-Action-Result) as a format for résumé bullets appears mainly in low-authority guides. It is primarily an interview-answer framework. [S] — [DevPlaybook](https://devplaybook.cc/blog/software-engineer-resume-50-bullet-points-star-format)

**Promotions and multiple roles at one company**
- Career guides agree on two formats:
  - **Stacked titles:** list the company once and stack each title beneath it, newest first, each with its own dates. Use this when duties stayed similar and the move was a step up in the same team; it also saves space.
  - **Separate blocks:** give each role its own block with its own bullets under the company heading when scope or responsibilities changed significantly.
  
  A company can appear twice if you left and returned. [S] — [TopResume](https://www.TopResume.com/career-advice/how-to-format-your-resume-for-multiple-jobs-at-one-company); [Monster](https://www.monster.com/career-advice/article/one-employer-multiple-jobs-resume); [VisualCV](https://www.visualcv.com/blog/promotions-on-resume/); [Coursera](https://www.coursera.org/articles/how-to-show-promotion-on-resume)

**Quantifying when junior**
- Orosz: "Use numbers to convey the impact of your work, or projects": team size, users, adoption, revenue. For less experience, cite tests, coverage and the user base of open-source projects you contributed to. [F] — [Orosz, dev.to](https://dev.to/gergelyorosz/six-principles-your-resume-should-follow-so-recruiters-will-read-it-3a0o)
- Tech Interview Handbook: quantify where possible and describe scope. [F] — [Tech Interview Handbook](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/resume/)
- Bock: give a baseline for comparison ("relative to Y"). [S] — [Business Insider India](https://www.businessinsider.in/google-hr-boss-says-this-is-the-key-to-a-perfect-resume/articleshow/43924179.cms)

**NDA, confidential and internal tools**
- Career guides converge on the following (these are career blogs, not legal advice):
  - Describe the technical pattern and the tools, languages and platforms used, without naming unreleased products.
  - Replace client names with a general description of the industry or client type.
  - Some guides label the entry as confidential.
  - Read the NDA to list what cannot be disclosed: client names, specs, financial metrics.
  - Share outcomes as relative changes (percentages) rather than proprietary figures.
  - Avoid vague "secret project" wording without context, which raises red flags.
  - Save the detail for interviews.
  
  [S] — [Resume Worded](https://resumeworded.com/blog/resume-nda); [Kickresume](https://www.kickresume.com/en/blog/resume-vs-non-disclosure-agreement/); [Teal](https://www.tealhq.com/post/confidential-resume); [techinterview.org](https://www.techinterview.org/post/3233474609/engineering-resume-experience-section-nda/)

### Inferences
- **Recommended web component, "Experience" (single column, reverse-chronological):**
  - **Header line:** `Role` · `Company` (plus a one-line descriptor such as "regional logistics company, ~300 employees" when the name is unknown abroad) · `City, MX / Remote` · `Mar 2025 – Present (1 yr 8 mo)`.
  - **Bullets:** 2–4 XYZ bullets per role on the main page. Juniors should keep the strongest 2–3 visible and put extra detail behind an expandable "More" or a "Case study →" link (progressive disclosure keeps the scan short).
  - **Tech tags:** 4–8 chips *after* the bullets, ordered by relevance, using the exact names found in job descriptions ("PostgreSQL", "TypeScript", "AWS Lambda"). Recruiters scan for keywords, and Orosz puts technologies last.
  - **Optional context line** before the bullets, for scope: team size, who the users are.
- **Timeline vs. cards vs. list:**
  - A vertical single-column list *is* a timeline and preserves F/layer-cake scanning. A decorative timeline rail is fine if headings stay bold and left-aligned.
  - Card grids suit **projects**, where readers compare items. Avoid multi-column card grids for **experience**: they break chronology and imitate the multi-column layouts that performed poorly in the Ladders study.
  - On mobile everything collapses to one column anyway.
- **Promotions.** Use one company block with stacked roles, for example "Software Engineer · Mar 2025–Present" above "Junior Developer (Intern) · Jun 2024–Feb 2025", each with its own 1–3 bullets. This makes the "history of growth" (gregjor) visible.
- **Junior metric proxies (be honest; use "~" and state the basis):**
  - people or teams using the tool, and how often it runs
  - records, files or requests processed
  - time saved ("from ~2 days to ~15 min")
  - errors or rework reduced
  - latency or cost
  - adoption (branches, departments)
  - test coverage, PRs merged, incidents resolved
  
  Use % for confidential absolute numbers.
- **Synthetic XYZ examples for a bilingual site** (illustrative only; not from any source):
  - Backend/data (EN): "Cut monthly report preparation from ~2 days to ~15 min (−95%) for a 6-person finance team by building a Python/pandas ETL that consolidates 4 CSV exports into a PostgreSQL view and a scheduled dashboard."
  - Backend/data (ES): "Reduje la preparación del reporte mensual de ~2 días a ~15 min (−95 %) para un equipo de finanzas de 6 personas, con un ETL en Python/pandas que consolida 4 exportaciones CSV en una vista de PostgreSQL y un tablero programado."
  - Full-stack (EN): "Shipped an internal inventory web app used daily by ~40 staff across 5 sites, replacing shared spreadsheets; cut stock-lookup time from minutes to seconds (Next.js, REST API, PostgreSQL, role-based auth)."
  - AI/automation (EN): "Automated triage of ~300 inbound emails/week with an LLM classifier that matched human labels on 92% of a 200-email test set, routing to 5 queues and saving ~6 staff-hours/week (Python, OpenAI API, n8n)."
- **NDA and internal tools on a public site:**
  - Name the employer only if allowed; otherwise use "Confidential — [industry] company".
  - Describe the problem, users, architecture and relative impact.
  - Never show real customer data. Use screenshots with synthetic or blurred data, or a short video with synthetic data.
  - Consider a clean-room re-implementation with synthetic data as a public repo, labelled as such.
  - Ask the employer for written permission before publishing screenshots.
- **Dates and terms.** Use one format per locale ("Mar 2025 – Present" / "mar 2025 – actualidad") and keep it identical to LinkedIn and the PDF. Title translations must not inflate seniority: "Practicante" = "Intern", "Becario" = "Intern/Trainee".

### Gaps
- No empirical study compares timeline vs. cards vs. list layouts for portfolio websites. The recommendation above is inferred from résumé and web eye-tracking.
- No authoritative number of bullets per role was found. Tech Interview Handbook says only "a few"; the commonly repeated "3–5 bullets" is unsourced here.
- No evidence-based guidance on how many tech tags per role, or on whether tags help recruiters (as opposed to ATS keyword matching).
- No legal source on what a typical Mexican employment confidentiality clause allows on a public portfolio. Check the actual contract.

## Q4. Education for juniors: placement and detail, and how to present a Mexican degree (e.g., Ingeniería en Sistemas Computacionales, cédula profesional) to international employers

### Takeaway
With under 3 years of experience, education can sit high on a US-style résumé (Tech Interview Handbook), but Mexican recruiters weigh experience far more than education in the first screen (92% vs. under 50%). Keep the section compact:
- degree, in English plus the original Spanish name
- institution, city and country
- dates or expected graduation
- GPA/promedio only if strong
- honors
- a capstone or thesis presented as a project

For international employers, present the degree as a 4–5-year bachelor's-level engineering degree. State título and cédula status accurately (a carta de pasante is not a completed degree). Know that formal equivalency (WES/ECE) matters mainly for visas and immigration, not for most remote contracts.

### Cited Findings
- Tech Interview Handbook (Aug 2026):
  - Put Education first if still in school or with under 3 years of experience; otherwise it "should not be prioritized above your work experience".
  - List GPA only if above 3.50/4.00 (or above 4.3/5).
  - Include leadership roles, societies, projects and awards.
  - Coursework is not addressed.
  
  [F] — [Tech Interview Handbook](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/resume/)
- OCC 2026 (Mexico): fewer than half of recruiters consider education level in the first screen, against 92% for experience. The headline frames it as "tu título espera" (your degree waits). [F] — [Publimetro / OCC 2026](https://www.publimetro.com.mx/noticias/2026/09/30/buscas-trabajo-en-mexico-tu-titulo-espera-92-prioriza-experiencia/)
- Evaluation of Mexican degrees (commercial and immigration-oriented sources; confirm with WES or ECE directly):
  - The **Título profesional** is the primary document WES uses to evaluate a licenciatura as a bachelor's equivalent. The **cédula profesional** is useful secondary verification.
  - Private-institution graduates may need proof of **RVOE** (SEP recognition at graduation).
  - Cédulas can be checked at cedulaprofesional.sep.gob.mx.
  
  [S] — [school-lookup blog](https://school-lookup-frontend.pages.dev/blog/how-to-verify-mexican-degrees)
- Program length matters. Mexican bachelor's programs run 3–5 years depending on the program, and 3-year degrees may not be treated as equivalent to a US bachelor's. A TSU (2-year Técnico Superior Universitario) is not equivalent to a 4-year licenciatura. A **carta de pasante** verifies completed coursework, not a completed degree. [S] — [MotaWord](https://www.motaword.com/it/blog/uscis-essentials-mexican-degree-assessment-for-immigration-lawyers); [Immigration Start Guide](https://immigrationstartguide.com/blog/wes-evaluation-mexican-degrees-canada); [RJ Immigration Law](https://rjimmigrationlaw.com/?p=6269)
- A 2017 USCIS AAO decision treated a Mexican título in electrical and mechanical engineering as the academic equivalent of a US bachelor's in engineering. This is a single case, not a rule. [S] — [USCIS decision PDF](https://uscis.gov/sites/default/files/err/B5%20-%20Members%20of%20the%20Professions%20holding%20Advanced%20Degrees%20or%20Aliens%20of%20Exceptional%20Ability/Decisions_Issued_in_2017/NOV282017_01B5203.pdf)
- Orosz: tailor ruthlessly and remove what doesn't help show fit. [F] — [Orosz, dev.to](https://dev.to/gergelyorosz/six-principles-your-resume-should-follow-so-recruiters-will-read-it-3a0o)

### Inferences
- **Placement:**
  - **Site:** Education goes after Experience and Projects whenever the candidate has any real work experience. Mexican recruiters prioritise experience, and remote employers care about proof of work. Above-the-fold it can be one line: "B.S. Computer Systems Engineering, [Institution] (2025)".
  - **EN CV:** for a 0–1-year candidate, Education can go near the top (Tech Interview Handbook rule). With 1–2 years of real experience, put Experience first.
- **English format, one line plus optional details:**
  `B.S. in Computer Systems Engineering (Ingeniería en Sistemas Computacionales) — [Institution], [City], Mexico · Aug 2021 – Jun 2025`
  - "4.5-year accredited engineering program": include the length only if it is 4+ years, to pre-empt equivalency doubts.
  - "Capstone/Thesis: [title] — [one-line result + link]".
  - "GPA 9.2/10" only if strong. Keep the native 0–10 scale rather than converting to 4.0, because conversions vary by evaluator.
  - Honors such as "Mención honorífica" translated as "Graduated with honors".
- **Spanish format:** `Ingeniería en Sistemas Computacionales — [Institución], [Ciudad] · 2021–2025 · Titulado(a); cédula profesional [emitida / en trámite]`. A cédula matters more for Mexican public-sector, regulated or consulting roles that ask for it.
- **Status honesty:**
  - "Degree completed; título in process", "Pasante (coursework completed)" and "Título and cédula issued" are different statuses. Do not write "B.S." alone if the título is not yet issued; use "B.S. (coursework completed, 2025; degree conferral in process)".
  - Don't publish the cédula number on the website. "Available on request" is enough, and it can be verified in SEP's registry.
- **Coursework and courses:**
  - List 4–6 relevant courses only if experience is thin or the courses map to the target profile: databases, distributed systems or networks for backend/data; ML or statistics for AI.
  - Online certificates (Platzi, Coursera, etc.) go in a separate "Courses" line, not under Education.
- **Equivalency evaluation:** WES/ECE is worth getting only when targeting relocation or visa processes, or employers that require it. For remote contractor or EOR roles it is rarely asked for. (Inference; see gaps.)

### Gaps
- The WENR "Education in Mexico" profile (wenr.wes.org) returned 404, so no authoritative WES statement on licenciatura equivalency, the 0–10 grading scale or passing grades was obtained.
- No source found on how US or European *employers* (as opposed to immigration authorities) treat Mexican degrees in hiring.
- No authoritative guidance on translating "Ingeniería en Sistemas Computacionales" (B.S. vs. B.Eng.; "Computer Systems Engineering" vs. "Computer Science").
- No data on how Mexican tech recruiters treat "pasante" vs. "titulado" status, or on the value of local credentials such as EGEL-CENEVAL results.

## Q5. Certifications and achievements: what carries weight vs. noise, and how to present hackathons, awards, scholarships, publications, open source, leadership and volunteering

### Takeaway
Certifications are a screening aid and a learning path, not proof of ability. In 2025 expert commentary the ones that carry weight are:
- cloud (AWS, Azure, GCP)
- infrastructure and DevOps (Kubernetes, Terraform, Docker)
- security (Security+)
- ML-platform certifications to a lesser degree

Core-language certificates are "niche", and multiple-choice "familiarity" certifications are seen as thin. A long list without built work hurts. Achievements should be quantified and contextualized ("Year | 1st of 50 teams | Event"). Open-source contributions merged into established projects are among the strongest junior signals.

### Cited Findings
- InfoWorld, "Do programming certifications still matter?" (Bob Violino, Oct 27, 2025). [F] — [InfoWorld](https://www.infoworld.com/article/3709508/do-programming-certifications-still-matter.html)
  - **Carry weight:**
    - "Cloud certifications from AWS, Azure, and GCP are incredibly valuable" (Watts, 8x8).
    - AWS, Kubernetes and Terraform are "the clearest path" for self-training in the AI era (Khan, Landing Point).
    - Kubernetes, Docker and Jenkins (Watts).
    - CompTIA Security+ and Certified Ethical Hacker "have become essential" (Miller, IFS).
    - Growing interest in ML, data science and TensorFlow certifications.
  - **Weak or noise:**
    - "Core programming certifications are still a bit niche" (Fuller, Codecademy).
    - Some certifications reflect "only familiarity rather than true expertise" (Riccio, Uplevel).
    - Some are "paper-thin" multiple-choice exams, and certifications age quickly (Khan).
    - Watts is wary of "a laundry list of certifications".
    - "A certification without experience doesn't carry much weight" (Watts).
    - Certifications "aren't seen as the noteworthy achievement they once were" (Miller).
  - **For juniors:**
    - Certifications are "especially helpful for people entering the field or shifting from adjacent roles" (Fuller).
    - They are a useful baseline signal early in a career, and one "might get someone an interview" (Miller).
    - They may help a résumé pass initial screening (Riccio).
- Tech Interview Handbook (Aug 2026):
  - Certifications and awards are optional sections at the end.
  - Include only relevant achievements and quantify them, using the format "[Year] | [Quantification] | [Competition]", e.g. "2016 | Best All-Round Product out of 50 teams | Facebook Hackathon".
  - Include at least 2 projects linked to viewable source such as GitHub.
  - The example cites an open-source maintainer role with scope and usage.
  
  [F] — [Tech Interview Handbook](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/resume/)
- Orosz: contribute to popular open-source projects and cite the project's user base where relevant. [F] — [Orosz, dev.to](https://dev.to/gergelyorosz/six-principles-your-resume-should-follow-so-recruiters-will-read-it-3a0o)
- HN (purvesta): open-source contributions to established projects carry weight because the code went through "a robust review process". [F] — [Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43134745)
- HackerRank 2025: developers weigh open-source contributions, community engagement and hackathons when evaluating *employers*, and students rank hackathons as the best way for a company to show its tech brand (28%). This shows the cultural weight of hackathons in the developer community; it is not a hiring criterion. [F] — [HackerRank 2025 DSR](https://www.hackerrank.com/reports/developer-skills-report-2025)
- Mexico: only about a quarter of candidates update their knowledge or certifications, and OCC advises keeping skills and certifications current. [F] — [Publimetro / OCC 2026](https://www.publimetro.com.mx/noticias/2026/09/30/buscas-trabajo-en-mexico-tu-titulo-espera-92-prioriza-experiencia/)
- Spanish-language lists put AWS and Azure among the most sought-after IT certifications. A Mexico-specific claim (AWS, Google Ads, PMP, CISSP and SAP as "most demanded") comes from a training vendor without verifiable data. [S] — [KeepCoding](https://keepcoding.io/blog/certificaciones-de-it-mas-reconocidas/); [CIO (ES)](https://www.cio.com/article/2140112/las-8-certificaciones-informaticas-mas-demandadas-en-la-actualidad.html); [IIENSTITU](https://www.iienstitu.com/es/blog/valor-certificaciones-mercado-laboral-latinoamericano)

### Inferences
- **Tiering for the three target profiles (show only what matches the target role):**
  - **Higher signal:** associate- or professional-level cloud certifications (AWS Developer or Solutions Architect Associate, Azure AZ-204, GCP Associate Cloud Engineer); CKAD or Terraform Associate for backend; security baselines (Security+) for backend roles touching auth or data.
  - **Data and AI:** a vendor ML or data certification can help if it is backed by a project that uses the same platform.
  - **Lower signal:** fundamentals-level certifications (cloud "practitioner" or "fundamentals"), MOOC completion certificates, and language-syntax certificates. Keep them in a compact "Courses" list, not in the hero section.
- **Presentation:**
  - Format: `Certification — Issuer — Mon YYYY (valid until Mon YYYY) — Verify ↗` (Credly or issuer link).
  - Pair each certification with proof: "Used in: [Project X] deployed on AWS ECS + RDS".
  - Remove expired certifications or mark them as expired.
  - Never list "in progress" certifications as earned.
- **Achievements (one section, reverse-chronological, quantified):**
  - **Hackathons:** `2025 | 2nd of 38 teams | [Event] — built [what] in 36 h with [stack] · Repo ↗ · Demo ↗`.
  - **Scholarships:** `[Scholarship] — [Sponsor], 2023 (selected 1 of ~N applicants)`, only if the selection rate is verifiable.
  - **Publications or theses:** full citation with link/DOI, plus one line on the result.
  - **Open source:** `[project] (★ N, used by …) — N PRs merged: [what changed]`, linking the merged-PR search URL. This is stronger than solo toy projects.
  - **Leadership:** e.g. student tech-chapter lead, with size and outcomes ("organized 6 workshops, ~250 attendees").
  - **Volunteering:** include if technical (teaching code, building for NGOs) or if it shows leadership. Keep it to 1 line.
- **The site as verifier.** Each achievement should link to verifiable evidence: certificate URL, event page, repo. This matches the post-AI shift toward verifiability.

### Gaps
- No rigorous 2025–2026 survey quantifies how specific certifications affect callback rates for junior software engineers. The "87% of IT hiring managers value certifications" figure traced only to vendor-style content (2024) and was not used.
- No data on how recruiters value hackathons, scholarships, publications or volunteering for junior engineers, in Mexico or abroad.
- No Mexico-specific recruiter data on certification value. Computrabajo, LinkedIn México and Platzi reports were not found in this research.
- Anecdotal forum claims that AWS associate certifications favour infrastructure roles over application development were not verified.

## Q6. Market differences: Mexico/LatAm vs. US/Europe (photo, age, address, CEFR language levels, salary expectations, timezone/availability, work authorization), and handling them on a bilingual site

### Takeaway
- **US, Canada, UK and most international remote employers:** no photo, no age or date of birth, no marital status, no full street address (city and country plus timezone is enough), and no salary on the CV.
- **Germany and some continental European countries:** photo and date of birth are still common, but declining at large international firms.
- **Mexico:** photos and personal data are still common but contested. LFT Art. 133 already forbids refusing a hire on grounds of age or gender, and a 2026 Senate initiative would make blind CVs mandatory (pending, not law). Mexican recruiters *do* screen on expected salary (67%) and place of residence (53%) in the first pass, so give a salary range where the job board or form asks, not on the public site.
- **English as differentiator:** Mexico's EF EPI 2025 score of 440 ranked 103rd of 123 ("very low" band). A verified B2–C1 level, plus timezone overlap, is a real advantage for remote US roles.
- **Bilingual site:** separate /en/ and /es/ URLs, hreflang, a visible language switcher, no automatic language or IP redirects, fully translated pages, and two CV variants.

### Cited Findings

For the UK, "a large minority (35%)" recommended not including a photo. [S] — [EMBL](https://www.embl.org/about/info/embl-fellows-career-service/blog/2019/08/what-to-include-in-your-cv/)

**Bilingual site (Google Search Central guidance)**
- URL structure: subdirectories (example.com/es/) are "Low maintenance (same host)". URL parameters are "Not recommended".
- Use hreflang annotations (or sitemaps) to connect language versions.
- "Avoid automatically redirecting" users based on presumed language, because it can stop users and search engines from seeing all versions. Add visible links or selectors to the other language versions.
- "Don't use IP analysis to adapt your content."
- Use one language per page, and avoid side-by-side translations. Translating only boilerplate while the main content stays in one language "can create a bad user experience".
- Google determines page language from visible content: "We don't use any code-level language information such as `lang` attributes".

### Inferences
- **Two CV/profile variants with identical facts** (titles, dates and metrics never differ):
  - **International / EN (US, Canada, UK, remote-first EU):**
    - No photo; no age, date of birth, marital status, CURP, RFC or NSS; no street address.
    - Header: `City, Mexico · UTC−6 (CDMX) · Open to remote (contractor or EOR) · Available for X–Y h overlap with US Eastern/Pacific`.
    - Write "No visa sponsorship needed for remote-from-Mexico roles" only if the posting asks about authorization, and never claim US work authorization you don't have.
    - Languages: `Spanish — native · English — C1 (EF SET, 2026: score NN/100)`. Pair the CEFR level with a named test or certificate (TOEFL, IELTS, Cambridge, or the free EF SET) because self-ratings are cheap.
    - No salary on the CV. For EU roles, never volunteer current salary; the EU ban targets employers asking, but volunteering is not needed.
  - **Mexico / ES:**
    - Default to no photo (the trend and the legal direction support it) unless a vacancy explicitly requests one. Keep a photo variant ready for vacancies or German employers that expect it.
    - No age, date of birth or CURP.
    - City and zone or municipality (53% of recruiters check residence) plus "Disponibilidad: presencial / híbrido / home office".
    - Put the expected salary range (gross monthly MXN) in the OCC/Computrabajo profile or application form when requested (67% screen on it), not on the public site.
    - Languages: "Inglés — C1 (certificado …)".
  - **Optional DE/EU variant:** add a professional photo and date of birth only for traditional German or Austrian employers that expect them.
- **Public website privacy baseline:** show only city or country, email (or a contact form), LinkedIn and GitHub. Never publish phone, full address, CURP, RFC, date of birth or cédula number. This also lowers identity-theft and scam risk.
- **Bilingual implementation:**
  - Use `/en/` and `/es/` subdirectories with reciprocal hreflang (including `x-default`).
  - Put a visible "EN | ES" switcher in the header that links to the *equivalent* page.
  - Do not auto-redirect by Accept-Language or IP. The root can be EN, the market where proof matters most, with a prominent "Español" link.
  - Translate everything (case studies included). Partially translated pages hurt UX per Google.
  - Set `<html lang>` correctly for screen readers, even though Google ignores it for language detection.
  - Hand-edit translations. Machine-translated or AI-flat text is a credibility risk given the AI-detection scepticism in Q1.
- **Locale-specific emphasis with the same facts:**
  - ES can foreground Mexican signals: título and cédula status, local certifications, "home office", MXN context.
  - EN foregrounds remote readiness: timezone overlap, async communication samples, English proof, and contractor or EOR readiness.
- **English as the main differentiator.** With Mexico's average English rated "very low", a candidate with demonstrable B2+/C1 English (written case studies, a 60–90 s intro video, recorded tech talk, open-source discussions in English) stands out for US remote roles. Timezone alignment is the second built-in advantage.

## Q7. Résumé/CV download on the site: PDF vs. web page, ATS considerations, one-page rule for juniors, keeping site and CV in sync

### Takeaway
Offer a text-based, single-column, one-page PDF CV in EN and ES, plus a printable HTML "CV" view. Generate both from the same data the website uses so dates, titles and metrics never drift from LinkedIn. ATS-safe means:
- real selectable text, not images
- standard section headings and fonts
- nothing in headers or footers
- no icon-only labels
- a plain-text copy test

Many modern ATS parse two-column PDFs, but vendor tests disagree and reading-order errors persist. The Ladders study also found columns hurt human scanning, so single column is the lower-risk choice.

### Cited Findings
- Tech Interview Handbook (Aug 2026):
  - "Use only 1 page for your resume", with 0.5-inch margins and at least 10 pt font.
  - Create it in Word or Google Docs and submit as a PDF whose text is "easily highlightable".
  - Use standard fonts (Arial, Calibri, Garamond) and avoid Photoshop, graphic tools and online résumé builders.
  - Put no content in headers or footers and no symbols in section headings.
  - Run a plain-text test for missing content, garbled characters or disorganized sections.
  - Some companies set ATS rules that auto-reject.
  
  [F] — [Tech Interview Handbook](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/resume/)
- Ladders [OLDER]: multiple columns, clutter and missing headers hurt human screening. [F] — [HR Dive](https://www.hrdive.com/news/eye-tracking-study-shows-recruiters-look-at-resumes-for-7-seconds/541582/)
- Two-column parsing, vendor research that conflicts:
  - Enhancv (357 tests) says virtually every modern ATS reads two-column résumés correctly and that the real problem is word order with older extraction methods.
  - ATS Verification's 2026 benchmark found two-column was the only one of six layouts with a critical flag (score 100→85).
  - A DEV Community test (4 parsers, 36 layouts) found column count did not predict the outcome; the order in which text is emitted did.
  
  All three have commercial incentives. [S] — [Enhancv](https://enhancv.com/blog/two-column-resume-ats-test/); [ATS Verification 2026](https://atsverification.com/blog/ats-parsing-benchmark-2026/); [DEV Community](https://dev.to/resumap/we-ran-the-same-resume-through-4-real-ats-parsers-in-36-layouts-same-text-different-parses-3mfc)
- HackerRank 2025: résumé filters block strong candidates, and filtering tools create false negatives (no figures). [F] — [HackerRank 2025 DSR](https://www.hackerrank.com/reports/developer-skills-report-2025)
- Orosz: keep a master résumé and tailor each application by cutting what is less relevant ("Be ruthless in removing things that don't help convey why you are a good fit"). [F] — [Orosz, dev.to](https://dev.to/gergelyorosz/six-principles-your-resume-should-follow-so-recruiters-will-read-it-3a0o)
- OCC 2026: only 36% of Mexican candidates tailor their CV per job, and 46% keep their online profile complete and up to date. [F] — [Publimetro / OCC 2026](https://www.publimetro.com.mx/noticias/2026/09/30/buscas-trabajo-en-mexico-tu-titulo-espera-92-prioriza-experiencia/)
- LinkedIn's Hiring Assistant sources and screens from LinkedIn profile data, so the profile is itself a screened document. [S] — [LinkedIn Newsroom](https://news.linkedin.com/2025/hiring-assistant-globally-available)
- Google: use separate URLs per language with hreflang, and one language per page. [F] — [Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/managing-multi-regional-sites)

### Inferences
- **What to offer on the site:**
  - A prominent "Download CV" button with EN and ES options linking to `/cv/Firstname-Lastname-CV-EN.pdf` and `-ES.pdf`. Keep file size small; text must be selectable and links clickable.
  - A `/en/cv` and `/es/cv` HTML page that prints cleanly (print stylesheet) and is indexable and accessible.
  - No form-gating and no image-only CV.
  - Show "Last updated: Mon YYYY" on both the PDF and the site.
- **Single source of truth:** keep experience, education, certifications and achievements in one structured data file (YAML/JSON, e.g. a JSON Resume-style schema) that drives both the website sections and the PDF generation (HTML→PDF via print CSS, or a Typst/LaTeX template). Update LinkedIn and the OCC/Computrabajo profile from the same file. This prevents the inconsistencies that fraud-wary screeners now notice.
- **Length:**
  - EN: one page for a 0–2-year junior (Tech Interview Handbook).
  - ES (Mexico): one page preferred. A second page is tolerable only if it carries real content such as an extra project or certifications, never personal data padding. This is unsourced for Mexico; see gaps.
- **ATS checklist for the PDF:**
  - single column
  - standard headings (Experience/Experiencia, Education/Educación, Skills/Habilidades, Projects/Proyectos, Certifications/Certificaciones)
  - dates in a consistent "Mon YYYY – Mon YYYY" format
  - contact details in the body, not the header or footer
  - no tables, icons, text boxes or skill bars
  - fonts embedded
  - copy-paste test into a plain-text editor
- **Tailoring:** keep 3 role-specific variants (Full-stack, Backend/Data, AI/Automation) that reorder bullets and tech tags. The website can show everything; the PDF should be targeted. Link the portfolio in the PDF header with a short URL.
- **Photo in PDFs:** none in EN. In ES, none by default, with an alternate version ready when a vacancy requests one (see Q6).

### Gaps
- No independent (non-vendor) 2025–2026 evidence was found on how specific ATS (Greenhouse, Lever, Workday, iCIMS, OCC's or Computrabajo's systems) parse PDFs, columns or icons.
- No data on whether recruiters prefer an HTML CV page or a PDF download on portfolio sites.
- No evidence-based guidance for 1 vs. 2 pages for Mexican or European junior CVs. An older (2018) ResumeGo experiment on two-page résumés was not verified here.
- No data on whether Mexican recruiters on OCC or Computrabajo rely on parsed CV fields or on the platform profile form. The OCC data suggests the profile matters (46% of candidates keep it updated), but it doesn't measure parsing.
